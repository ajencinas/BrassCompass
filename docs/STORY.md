# The Brass Compass: story bible

The single source of truth for backstory, cast, voices and act beats. When a line in the game
disagrees with this file, the line is wrong. The scene-by-scene script is
[`SCRIPT.md`](SCRIPT.md); the build plan is [`GAME_PLAN.md`](GAME_PLAN.md).

## Logline

A customs clerk who has never been further than the end of the pier signs on with a licensed
pirate crew to find his father's lost ship, falls for the lookout who calls him "cargo", and
uncovers the politest crime in the Vela Straits.

## Tone

**A romantic pirate comedy.** Moonlight in the rigging and paperwork in triplicate. The jokes
come from character and bureaucracy; the heart comes from people who were written down as lost
and refused to stay that way. Nothing supernatural: every mystery has a ledger entry behind it.
The romance is played straight and sweet, and the comedy never punctures it at the moment it
matters.

## Backstory (what really happened)

**The compass.** Orrin Fenn, Port Vela instrument maker and Fenn's grandfather, found the
Needle: the one straight channel through the Vela reef, lined up on an iron-rich rock the old
sailors call the Old Nail. He built a compass that points at the Old Nail instead of north.
Anywhere but the Straits it is useless, which is why nobody ever stole it. Captain Ansel Fenn
sailed the Arcadia by it and made her the fastest ship on the Straits run without telling a soul
how.

**Fenn's mother.** Lise Fenn, a sailmaker, died when Fenn was six. Ansel stitched her initials
into every Arcadia flag. Fenn doesn't know that until he sees flag number fifteen.

**The trick ("the Weather").** Silas Cray of Cray & Co., factors, ships cargo and underwrites
its insurance. He also holds a harbour licence for a quarantine station on Gullet Key, a low
island inside the reef. His men, nicknamed the Weather, burn kelp in iron braziers to raise fog
over the reef passage, row out under a yellow quarantine flag, and politely order each crew into
the boats "for inspection". They strip the cargo and set the empty hull adrift with sails set.
No blood, no boats, no bodies: just a hull, which the insurers pay out on "for weather". Cray
resells the cargo as salvage. *Dead men cause inquests. Missing men cause a line in a ledger.*
The crews are alive on Gullet Key, raking salt, held by the quarantine.

**The ledger.** For every missing ship Cray's office pays the harbour "quarantine dues", and
Harbourmaster Bramwick enters it as lost at sea, sometimes before it is overdue. He was paid
to, and has hated himself for three years. The Arcadia was entered three days before she was
late: only the man who arranged it could have known.

**The Arcadia.** Six ships in three years; the Arcadia was the first. When the Weather came,
Ansel refused the boats, ran for the Needle through the fog without his compass (he had left it
on the hall table because Cray had twice tried to buy it) and put her on the reef inside the
lagoon. She is still there, heeled over, masts up. The Weather took the crew off anyway.

**The flags.** Ansel lives in the salt camp. Every few dawns at low tide he crosses the reef
flats to the wreck and raises a home-made Arcadia flag, sewn from shirts, with an L stitched in
the corner. The Weather tear it down; he sews another. He is on number fifteen.

**Rook.** The Arcadia's navigator. The Weather took him off first, paid him and put him aboard a
passing fruit boat, because a navigator who can find his way back is a liability. He has never
opened the purse. It is sealed with Cray's cloud. The Arcadia's manifest lists every hand as lost
except "R. Rook, navigator: discharged at sea."

**Bramwick and Marta.** Thirty years of cards at the Salty Keel. The "IOU to Marta, one game of
cards, unpaid" in his drawer is a love letter written as a debt so he'd have an excuse to keep it
("and everything else", in very small writing). Paying it would mean it's over, so he never has.

**Captain Vane.** Once rich. Cray's insurance office refused her honest storm claim on a
technicality the same year it paid the ghost ships in full, and ruined her. She takes Cray
charters now because nobody else will hire her; the Marrow's deck cargo is crates of kelp for
the "quarantine station", stencilled QUARANTINE STN, G.K. and painted over with Cray's cloud.
Her navigator and oldest friend, Pell, died last winter in his hammock, mid-sentence. She steers
Pell's line north of the reef because Pell never dared the south. Under her pillow: a romance
novel, using the insurers' rejection letter as a bookmark.

## The romance (Fenn and Quill)

A swashbuckling slow burn told in banter. She is the pirate; he is the least piratical man in
Port Vela. She sweeps him off his feet (once literally); he wins her by taking her ambition
seriously enough to write it down. Affection is a hidden score raised by kind, funny or brave
choices. It changes lines and the ending, never whether you can finish.

1. **Act I: rumour.** Marta: the Marrow's lookout "can spot a lie at two miles and a compliment
   at none."
2. **Act II: meeting.** She blocks his way: "cargo with opinions". He returns her spyglass; she
   is annoyed to be grateful.
3. **Act II: the night watch** (optional, after she becomes acting navigator). The crow's nest
   under the stars. She teaches him three stars; he writes "Quill, navigator" in the log because
   writing a thing down is how you make it true. She says that's the stupidest thing she's ever
   heard, and asks him to underline it.
4. **Act II: the fog.** She takes the wheel while he calls bearings from the bow. First time
   they work as a team; first time she shouts "Good!" at him.
5. **Act III: the boat.** She rows him to the wreck and refuses to wait with the boat.
6. **Act III: the log.** When Fenn can't read the entry about his mother, Quill reads it aloud
   to him.
7. **Act III: the halyard.** Cornered at the Weather Works, she swings them both across the
   braziers on a halyard. "Hold on to me, cargo." "I am holding on to you." "Good. Keep doing
   that."
8. **Act III: the cell.** Pike locks Quill up; Fenn gets her out with a form she didn't know
   existed. She kisses him. Ansel, arriving: "Ah. Good. I'll come back."
9. **Act IV: the dock.** The Marrow must sail back out at dawn whatever happens in Port Vela.
   The farewell, short and true. She calls him "Fenn" for the first time.
10. **Ending: the choice.** Turn pirate (sign the Marrow's articles as purser and sail beside
    her) or keep the desk (keep the ledger honest; she sails into Port Vela every season with a
    new star to show him). Both are happy.

## Four acts

1. **A Licence to Sail** (Port Vela, night). Get a licence by stamping it yourself; prove Cray's
   claim on the last berth is a fraud (the manifest from the chandler, the truth from the
   ledger). Bramwick confesses and voids the claim.
2. **The Vela Straits** (aboard the Marrow, dawn to fog). Get past Quill; win Vane's three
   terms (a navigator who'll swear to the line, a crew that'll sail it, and a reason Pell would
   have); steer the Needle through the fog by compass. Ends on a wreck flying a homemade flag.
3. **Tidy Weather** (the reef and Gullet Key). Board the Arcadia; read Ansel's log; beat the
   fake quarantine with properly smoked, stamped and countersigned paperwork; lift the fog; find
   Ansel; signal the Marrow. Ends as Cray's cutter races home.
4. **All Hands Returned** (Port Vela, night). Cray has had Bramwick suspended and will write the
   Marrow down as lost at the dawn bell. Gather the evidence, hold an inquiry at the Salty Keel
   with Marta presiding, reinstate Bramwick, correct the ledger, and choose: pirate or desk.

## Facts to keep straight

- The Marrow sails **at dawn**; Act I is the night before. Act IV is the night after the return.
- "Six ships in three years, the Arcadia first."
- Cray's company is **Cray & Co., factors**; its seal is a cloud.
- The quarantine flag is **yellow**; the fake fog is **kelp smoke**.
- The compass points at the **Old Nail**, which lines up the **Needle**.
- Ansel's flags carry an **L** for Lise.
