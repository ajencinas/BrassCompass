# The Brass Compass: The Father and the Fog — story bible

The canon for the reboot. [`REBOOT.md`](REBOOT.md) is the showrunner spec and wins any argument
about names, flags, rooms or acts; this file is the readable bible for writers, artists and
reviewers. The screenplay is generated from the live `.adv` scripts in
[`FULL_SCRIPT.md`](FULL_SCRIPT.md); the scripts themselves live in
[`src/content/scripts/`](../src/content/scripts/) and are the only source for what the game says.

The old voyage is archived in `src/content/old-scripts/` and is never loaded. Do not import its
plot, its institutions or its vocabulary.

## Logline

A cartographer's apprentice returns to Dreadwater Isle to find his father, missing three months in
the permanent fog offshore. The only captain willing to sail is the man everyone blames for the
disappearances — and the fog is hiding something older than either of them.

## Tone

A fog-bound pirate adventure with dry humour: **warm on the island, eerie in the fog, theatrical
whenever Vex is on screen.** The jokes come from character — Vex's vanity, the islanders'
superstitions, Fenn being the least piratey man on Dreadwater. The scares come from weather,
sound and absence, never from gore.

### Family-friendly rules

- No nudity, no sexual content, no innuendo a ten-year-old would ask about, no objectifying
  camera or dialogue, no gore.
- Pirate peril, ghosts, drinking and cards are fine. The dead are **sad and strange, never
  disgusting**.
- Romance is chaste: banter, a held hand, at most one kiss. There is warmth between Fenn and
  Quill here — a shared watch, an argument that turns into trust — and no more than that on the
  page.
- Attractive character design means charismatic and good-looking, fully dressed. Not sexy.
- **No bureaucracy plot.** The mystery is the ghost and the fog. Fenn is a cartographer: maps,
  charts and bearings, not forms. Vex's crimes are salvage and cowardice, not accounting.
- Every spoken line is plain: one idea per line, about twenty words or fewer, and never over 140
  characters. Choices are ten words or fewer. See [`DIALOGUE_STYLE.md`](DIALOGUE_STYLE.md).

## Backstory (what really happened)

### Dreadwater and the Shroud

Dreadwater Isle was a pirate port, and still would be if the ships kept coming. The harbour town
is Dreadwater; the houses climb to Upper Town; the tavern is the Salty Keel. Six ships have gone
in three years. Five came back empty — sails set, wheel tied off, no crew. The sixth was Elias
Fenn's *Arcadia*, three months ago, and it never came back at all.

A few miles offshore sits **the Shroud**: a permanent fog bank that does not move with the
weather. Ships that enter it vanish. The island has learned to call the disappearances weather,
because the alternative is worse.

### The Grey Lantern

The Shroud is not weather. It is the wake of **the Grey Lantern**, a pirate ship lost eighty years
ago. Her captain, **Mordant**, refused to sink. The ship takes other ships, and the crews of the
taken stand their old watches still — not rotting, not living, keeping time in the fog. The fog
grows with every ship the Lantern takes.

Mordant is not a monster in the gory sense. He is ancient, courteous and territorial. He keeps
taking ships because a ship that stops is a ship that sinks, and he keeps the crews because a
ship needs hands. He is afraid of the water and tired of being afraid; the horror is that he is
polite about it.

### Elias Fenn

Elias Fenn, captain of the *Arcadia*, sailed into the Shroud to chart a way through it. The
*Arcadia* went down out past the reef. Elias survived, alone, on the fog rock inside the Shroud.
He has been there three months and four days, charting the Lantern's road: west, then north, then
back into the fog, every time, without hurry.

He is cheerful about catastrophe and apologises for the wrong things — a borrowed coat, a missed
birthday, his beard — and only at the end for the thing that matters. He has worked out the three
rules that keep a ship alive in the Shroud, and he has a plan, though he is less sure everyone
comes back.

### Vex, and what he did

**Captain Vex** is master of the *Marrow*, the only ship still sailing from Dreadwater. He works
the fog's edge, salvages what floats out, and sells it back to the island. The island blames him
for the disappearances. He lets them believe it, because fear keeps his prices high.

He saw Elias once, three weeks ago, on the reef: a man waving a lantern. Vex turned his light
away and told himself the fog was too thick. He has been telling himself that ever since. He does not become a hero. He stays selfish, vain and useful, and the trust Fenn gives him
— or withholds — decides what he does at the end.

### The three rules

Elias's discovery, and the spine of the last two acts:

1. **The bell.** The Lantern hunts by sound. A ship's bell calls it in. Silence the bell and it
   loses you for a while; cut the bell loose and the fog loses its voice.
2. **The lantern.** The great lantern shows it where the ship is. Light draws it, and darkness
   blinds it. Put the great lantern out and the fog cannot see you.
3. **The name.** The Lantern cannot take a ship whose crew speaks her captain's name. The name is
   **Mordant**, cut into a brass plate in the captain's cabin: `GREY LANTERN — M. MORDANT, MASTER`.

Breaking the curse means all three: cut the bell loose, douse the great lantern, and speak
Mordant's name. Then the choice is what to do with the dead.

### Voices in practice

- **Fenn** narrates in the first person, in short declaratives. He notices the practical detail
  before the feeling, then the feeling arrives anyway: *"The harbour is quiet. Even the gulls have
  gone to bed."* He is not a coward and not a hero; he is the man who writes it down.
- **Vex** talks in performance. He answers a question with a better line, then the true line, then
  a price. He is funny because he is almost honest: *"A thief with a ship. The only kind that
  sails."*
- **Elias** is warm and distracted, and apologises for the wrong things first. The reunion is the
  plainest writing in the game: short sentences, beats, silence.
- **Quill** is blunt, fast and competitive. She corrects your wording. Her tenderness arrives as
  a practical instruction: *"Hold that. Don't wring it out. When I'm up, pass it."*
- **Hobb** speaks once per scene if he can. His feelings are in what he does with his hands.
- **Dorrit** is fussy and superstitious, and his courage is domestic: forty spoons, a pot that
  stays on, a kitchen offered to a stranger.
- **Marta** keeps the island's stories and charges for the rum. She is the one who says the plain
  thing when everyone else is being careful.
- **Bramwick** recites the harbour's rules because they are the only thing he can still hold. He
  wrote Elias down as lost in one line, and has read that line every morning since.
- **Rook** is wry about himself because the alternative is being serious about himself. He came
  back when nobody else did, and it is a warning, not a boast.
- **Pike** is a crook with a clipboard and a professional manner. He is never fought; he is
  out-talked, and he would rather write it off than make a scene.
- **Mordant** is courteous, patient and territorial. He never raises his voice and never
  threatens. He asks what you have done to his ship, and he means it.

### The island

Nell sells rope and dried fish and has already decided what you are going to buy. Young Tam counts
the ships from the corner and is Hobb's nephew; his father, Tam Hobb, sailed on the *Arcadia* and
is now the hand on the Grey Lantern's bell. Old Onn sweeps cobbles that are already clean and
remembers every man the street has lost.

## Four acts

### Act I — The Return

Fenn lands at Dreadwater. His father's room at the Salty Keel is untouched; the harbour is half
empty; nobody will talk about the fog. He needs a ship and a crew.

Beats:

1. **Arrival.** The boatman leaves Fenn on the harbour quay, and he walks the quay past the harbourmaster's house to the harbour mouth. The *Marrow* is the only ship with her gear
   aloft; the other berths are empty and the lamps are lit for nobody.
2. **Bramwick.** The harbourmaster confirms the plain facts: Elias is three months gone, no wreck,
   no boat, no body. The only ship still sailing is Vex's *Marrow*. Marta at the Salty Keel knows
   everyone; Vex keeps the counting house on Wreckers' Lane.
3. **The Salty Keel.** Marta, Old Rook in the corner, Dorrit in the kitchen, the regulars at the
   long table. The island is frightened and pretending not to be.
4. **The rudder.** Vex will not sail. Pike's wreckers have taken his spare rudder; bring it back
   and he will talk. Pike keeps the lane, a clipboard and a shed full of other people's stores.
5. **Dorrit's name.** Stores are missing from the boat shed and Pike's crew blames Dorrit. The
   real thief is Wen, a fog survivor hiding behind the crates. He moved the stores because Pike
   paid him, and he knows where the rudder is. Tell Pike the man talked; Pike returns the rudder
   and writes it off as weather. Dorrit is cleared, packs forty spoons and joins.
6. **Quill's compass.** Vex holds Quill's lucky compass against a debt. Part of the deal is its
   return. Take it to Quill at the shipwright's and she joins as navigator.
7. **Rook's log.** Rook will talk if Fenn fetches his old log from the keeper's store at the
   lighthouse. The last page is a list of names. Rook gives the first rule — it hunts by sound —
   and the chart of the fog's edge that Elias folded into the back of the log.
8. **Hobb.** The *Marrow*'s mast band is seized. Take the crowbar from the shipwright's counter,
   free the band, and Hobb joins. His brother Tam sailed on the *Arcadia* and has not come home.
9. **The deal.** Vex offers his ship, his rules, half the salvage and himself as captain. At the
   harbour mouth he takes the oars, and the *Marrow* sails.

Optional: Upper Town (Nell, young Tam, Old Onn, the window of Elias's room), and the lighthouse
yard. The regulars, the well and the father's room are texture, not gates.

### Act II — The Crew

1. **Fit-out.** Deck, helm, captain's cabin, crew deck, crow's nest, stern gallery. Each of the
   crew says, in their own way, why they came.
2. **The first attempt.** Vex sends Fenn to the bow to "call the water". The bell rings with no
   hand on the rope; the bow lantern dies; something vast passes under the hull. Vex's order is
   silence and no lights, and that is what saves them. **Rule two:** it hunts light.
3. **The hold.** Below decks, the crew finds Vex's salvage: barrels and a sea-chest marked
   CORMORANT, KESTREL, ARCADIA — cargo from the ships the fog took, sold back to the island.
   The crew confronts Vex at the helm. He admits it, refuses to turn back, and takes the *Marrow*
   deeper to prove it is only weather.
4. **The Lantern.** The fog takes the *Marrow*. Fenn ends up in the longboat with the crew, the
   wreck behind them and the fog closing its door. **Rule three** is waiting on the reef.

### Act III — The Shroud

1. **The fog flats.** The longboat beaches on the flats. The *Marrow* is going down. Vex admits he
   saw Elias on the reef, waving a lantern, and turned away. The crew wants answers; he has none
   that help.
2. **The sunken wreck.** An old victim of the Lantern, heeled over on the reef. Hobb hears a bell
   across the flats — three notes, then one — and knows it is Tam. On the wreck he works a broken
   bow timber free: a brass plate, `M. MORDANT, MASTER`. **The name.** He also sets the plan for
   the bell.
3. **The castaway camp.** Tents, a washing line, a hut locked from the outside, salt pans raked
   into squares — and Elias, alive, with his chart. The reunion is quiet. Elias gives the three
   rules, the Lantern's route and the plan: cut the bell, douse the great lantern, speak the name.
   Hobb hears the bell again. His brother Tam is the one ringing it.
4. **The far flats.** Optional: Quill at the end of the boardwalk, with her glass and the truth
   about the fog's edge.
5. **The reef gate.** Vex holds the gate until the plan is real: Elias found, the name known,
   Hobb's plan heard. Then the longboat goes into the water, the gate swings out, and the Grey
   Lantern comes out of the fog to meet them.

### Act IV — The Grey Lantern

Three objectives, in any order:

- **The bell** (Hobb). Climb the rigging and cut the bell loose. Tam Hobb is the hand on the rope:
  thin, tired and alive, still keeping the watch. The clapper comes away; the bell hangs dumb.
- **The lantern** (Quill). Carry the wet oiled canvas up the stern and smother the great green
  lantern. No air, no flame; no flame, no fog.
- **The name** (Elias). In the captain's cabin, set the brass plate on the desk and read it. Elias
  says the name: **Mordant**. The cold at the end of the cabin turns toward them.

Then Mordant. He is courteous, tired and territorial. He explains what he has done to keep his
ship afloat and why the crews stay at their posts. He does not threaten; he waits.

Before the last choice, Vex asks for something to hold on to — the boat, the rope, or Fenn's word.
The answer sets `vex_trust`.

## The ghost's rules (player-facing)

1. **It hunts by sound.** The bell calls a ship in. Keep quiet and it loses you for a while. Cut
   the bell loose and it loses its voice.
2. **It hunts by light.** The great lantern shows it where the ship is. Put out the light and the
   fog goes blind. Wet canvas over the glass is enough.
3. **It cannot take a ship whose crew speaks her captain's name.** The name is Mordant, on the plate in
   the cabin. Say it and the ship is no longer his to take.

All three together break the curse. The order the player does them in does not matter; the choice
at the end does.

## Endings

Both endings are earned and neither is a fail state. One is mercy; one is survival.

### The Fog Lifts (`ending_fog_lifts`)

Ring the bell for the dead, speak Mordant's name, and let the crews go. The Grey Lantern settles
into the sea like a tired sail; the fog thins to mist and then to weather. Elias comes home with
the chart under his arm. Quill charts the Shroud and calls the first clear channel Quill's Run.
Hobb and his brother Tam come home together; Dorrit feeds every crew that comes in; Bramwick meets every boat
at the pier; Marta hangs a new story over the Salty Keel's fire, and this one has an ending. The
bell hangs in the harbour and rings for the dead on the first clear morning of the year.

### The Long Way Home (`ending_long_way`)

Take the living and run. The longboat gets out; the dead stay at their posts and the fog remains.
Quill brings them home on a heading she invents on the spot and never says how close it was. The
island takes them back; the fishing boats still do not go past the reef. The blank half of the
chart hangs in the Salty Keel, and Marta says that is the honest part. On quiet nights the bell
rings across the water, and Fenn answers it — which is not the same as going back.

### Vex's fate (`vex_trust`)

Vex is never the hero. If Fenn trusted him:

- **Fog lifts:** he stays and rebuilds the harbour. The island calls it the first honest thing he
  has done.
- **Long way:** he takes the salvage and leaves, but leaves the longboat at the reef for Elias and
  calls it a debt worth collecting. Elias comes home a month later.

If Fenn trusted him with nothing:

- **Fog lifts:** he tells the island he lifted the fog. Nobody believes him. He tells it anyway,
  with a new coat.
- **Long way:** he takes the salvage and the credit. The ones left behind are not in his story,
  and Elias stays on the fog rock to finish the chart.

## The chart and the ship's log

Two different things share a name, and the UI should keep them distinct:

- **The Chart** (`J`, the old notes system) is what Fenn has learned and pinned in order: the
  plan at the top, then the entries. The item **Elias's chart** is his father's partial map of
  the Shroud — a physical thing in the inventory, not the UI page.
- **The Ship's Log** is the optional layer: the places, people and choices a player may have
  missed, shown after the ending and tallied on the title. It is not a gate and it is not a
  checklist of obligations.

Keep the existing engine identifiers where the code needs them; the player-facing words are
**Chart** and **Ship's log**.

Facts:

- The game is **The Brass Compass**; this story is **The Father and the Fog**; the tagline is
  **a tale of Dreadwater Isle**.
- Four acts, four titles: **The Return**, **The Crew**, **The Shroud**, **The Grey Lantern**.
- The game is navigated as Fenn's chart: the island, then the area (the harbour, upper town, the
  Marrow, the Shroud, the Grey Lantern), then the room. Each act opens on its area card.
- 25 rooms. The *Quay* links the harbour to the harbour mouth; the *Marrow* is Vex's ship; the Grey Lantern is Mordant's.
- Elias has been gone three months and four days. The *Arcadia* went down out past the reef.
- The Grey Lantern is eighty years old. Its crew are the vanished sailors, still at their posts.
- The name is **Mordant**. The plate reads `GREY LANTERN — M. MORDANT, MASTER`.
- The fog is the Lantern's wake and grows with every ship it takes.
- Vex saw Elias and did not help. He is never redeemed and never the hero.
- The dead are sad and strange, never disgusting. No one is eaten; nothing is gory.
- There is no bureaucracy plot and no fail state. The only clock is the one the player brings.
