# Reboot: The Father and the Fog — showrunner spec

This file is authoritative for the reboot. Every agent works from it: names, flags, acts,
voices, rooms, items and the family-friendly rules are fixed here unless the coordinator says
otherwise. The old Port Vela / insurance story is gone; do not reuse its plot, its ledger
scheme, or its characters' roles (the *art* is reused where noted).

## Logline

A cartographer's apprentice returns to Dreadwater Isle to find his father, missing three months
in the permanent fog offshore. The only captain willing to sail is the man everyone blames for
the disappearances — and the fog is hiding something older than either of them.

## Tone and rules

- A fog-bound pirate adventure with dry humour: warm on the island, eerie in the fog, theatrical
  whenever Vex is on screen.
- **Family friendly.** No nudity, no sexual content, no innuendo a ten-year-old would ask about,
  no objectifying camera or dialogue, no gore. Pirate peril, ghosts, drinking and cards are fine;
  the dead are sad and strange, never disgusting. Romance is chaste: banter, a held hand, one
  kiss. Attractive character design means charismatic and good-looking, not sexy.
- **No paperwork plot.** No insurance, no ledger fraud, no forms. The mystery is the ghost and
  the fog. Fenn is a cartographer (maps, not ledgers); his book is a chart.
- The ghost is real. The twist is that the island blamed Vex, and the thing in the fog is older
  and worse.
- Every spoken line is plain: one idea per line, ≤ ~20 words, ≤140 characters. Choices ≤10 words.
  (See docs/DIALOGUE_STYLE.md; it still applies.)

## Canon

- **Dreadwater Isle.** A pirate port going quiet. The harbour town is Dreadwater; Upper Town is
  the island's houses; the Salty Keel is the tavern.
- **The Shroud.** A permanent fog bank a few miles offshore. Ships that enter vanish. The fog is
  the wake of the Grey Lantern and grows with every ship it takes.
- **The Grey Lantern.** A pirate ship lost eighty years ago. Its captain, **Mordant**, refused to
  sink. It takes ships and crews to keep itself afloat. Its crew are the vanished sailors, still
  at their posts: not rotting, not living.
- **Three rules** (Elias's discovery): the Lantern hunts by **bell** (sound) and **lantern**
  (light), and it cannot take a ship whose captain **speaks its name**. Breaking the curse means
  cutting the bell loose, dousing the great lantern, and speaking Mordant's name.
- **Elias Fenn.** Fenn's father, captain of the *Arcadia* (keep the name), lost three months ago.
  Alive on the fog rock, charting the Lantern's route. Cheerful about catastrophe, apologises for
  the wrong things.
- **Captain Vex.** Master of the *Marrow*, the only captain still sailing. The island blames him
  for the disappearances; he lets them, because fear keeps his salvage prices high. Theatrical,
  vain, selfish, more competent than he looks. He saw Elias once and did not help him. He does not
  become a hero; he stays selfish and useful.
- **The Marrow.** Vex's ship (reuse all the painted ship interiors). The player's crew sails on
  her; the Lantern takes her in Act II.

## Four acts

### Act I — The Return

Fenn lands at Dreadwater. His father's room at the Salty Keel is untouched; the harbour is half
empty; nobody will talk about the fog. He needs a ship and a crew.

Beats:
1. Arrival at the harbour; Bramwick meets him; the *Marrow* is laid up; Vex refuses to sail.
2. The Salty Keel: Marta; Rook drinking in the corner; Quill the lookout; the island's fear.
3. **Job — the rudder:** Pike's wreckers stole Vex's spare rudder. Recover it (bluff/trade, no
   combat). Reward: Vex agrees to talk.
4. **Job — Dorrit's name:** stores are missing; Dorrit is blamed. Find the real thief (a fog
   survivor hiding in the boat shed). Reward: Dorrit joins; he saw the last crew taken.
5. **Job — Quill's compass:** Quill's lucky compass is held by Vex against a debt. Get it back
   (trade the rudder's recovery, or find Vex's strongbox). Reward: Quill joins.
6. **Job — Rook's story:** Rook will talk if Fenn brings him his old log from the lighthouse.
   He tells rule one (it hunts by sound) and draws the fog's edge. Reward: the chart.
7. Hobb joins once the Marrow can sail (he repairs the mast with the crowbar); he wants his
   brother Tam found.
8. Vex offers the deal: his ship, his chart, half the salvage, and he captains. Act ends sailing.

### Act II — The Crew

1. Fit-out scenes on the Marrow: Quill at the nest, Dorrit below, Hobb at the mast, Vex at the
   helm. Each says why they came.
2. First attempt into the Shroud: the bell rings with no hand; the great lantern light dies; the
   Lantern passes under the hull. They escape by silence and dark. Rule two (it hunts by light).
3. Vex's hold is found full of salvage from the lost ships. The crew confronts him.
4. Vex takes the Marrow into the fog to prove himself; the Lantern takes her; everyone ends up
   on the wreck/longboat at the fog rock. Rule three hinted in the wreck's nameplate.

### Act III — The Shroud

1. Stranded; the crew at each other's throats; Vex admits he saw Elias and did nothing.
2. The fog rock and castaway camp: Elias alive, charting. The three rules; the Lantern's route.
3. Hobb hears the bell and knows it is Tam ringing it. The plan: board the Lantern, cut the bell,
   douse the lantern, speak the name.
4. Board the Grey Lantern (end of act).

### Act IV — The Grey Lantern

Three objectives, any order:
- **The bell** (Hobb): climb the bell and cut it loose; Tam is the one ringing it. Free him.
- **The lantern** (Quill): reach the great lantern in the rigging and douse it with wet canvas.
- **The name** (Elias): find Mordant's name on the nameplate in the captain's cabin.

Then Mordant. The choice:
- **Lay them to rest:** ring the bell for the dead, speak the name, free the crews; the fog lifts;
  Elias comes home; the island sails again.
- **Take the living and run:** escape the fog; the dead stay; the fog remains; Elias stays to
  finish the chart (or comes home if the trust flag is high).

Vex's fate (flag `vex_trust`): stays to rebuild the harbour / leaves with the salvage / tries to
take the credit. He is never the hero.
