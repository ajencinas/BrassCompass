# Dialogue style: plain speech, earned knowledge, warmth

This is the house style for every spoken line, choice label and in-world note in *The Father and
the Fog*. It exists because the first draft was written like a novel: nearly every line was a
constructed epigram, and characters referred to Fenn's father before any scene had established
that they knew him.

**The test for every line:** a player reads it once, at speed, and understands it. The joke (if
there is one) lands from the situation or the character, not from the sentence's architecture.

## Rules

1. **One idea per line.** Aim for 25 words or fewer. Split anything over ~140 characters into two
   lines (`node ... -> next`) rather than one long sentence.
2. **Plain words.** If a bright twelve-year-old would not say it, rewrite it. No "one-syllable
   answers", no "the more profitable hobby", no "a debt with better manners".
3. **One comparison per ten lines, at most.** Prefer concrete nouns and verbs. Cut stacked
   similes and metaphors; the painted art is doing the describing.
4. **No asides inside dialogue.** Em-dashes, semicolons and parenthetical jokes in a spoken line
   make it hard to read aloud. Use two short lines instead.
5. **Jokes come from character, not vocabulary.** A blunt answer, an official phrase misapplied,
   a specific detail, an understatement. If a line needs re-reading to be funny, it is not funny.
6. **Choices are short and plain.** Ten words or fewer, first person, a verb you would actually
   say. No condition explanations, no stacked clauses, no jokes that hide the action.
   Good: `Ask about the Marrow.` / `Show him the compass.` / `Take the rope.`
   Bad: `"And the fog: something did call the Marrow's name, through a speaking-trumpet.
   Nobody an—"`
7. **Do not explain the joke.** If the first sentence lands, cut the second.
8. **Keep the voices.** Fenn: polite, precise, deadpan. Quill: blunt, quick, competitive. Vex:
   charming, evasive, never quite lying. Marta: warm, sharp. Bramwick: pompous, kind, guilty.
   Rook: wry, self-mocking. Hobb: few words. Dorrit: hot-tempered, generous, a cook's certainty.
   Pike: jobsworth. Nell: she sells news, not answers. Onn: old, direct, no patience for comfort.
   Elias: cheerful about catastrophe, apologises for the wrong things. Mordant: courteous, tired,
   never raises his voice. The plain style must not flatten these into one narrator.

## Knowledge: who knows what, and how they show it

Dreadwater Isle is a small harbour town in the Shroud. Fenn is a cartographer's apprentice; his
father **Elias Fenn** sailed into the fog three months ago on the *Arcadia* and did not come back.
It is public knowledge that Fenn is Elias's son, and that the *Arcadia* was lost. That is allowed —
but the game must say so once, early, and characters must show *how* they know.

| Character | Knows? | How it must read on the page |
|---|---|---|
| Bramwick | Elias, the *Arcadia*, the harbour's losses | He has the harbour's papers and an old guilt; he says so plainly. |
| Marta | Elias, his room, Dorrit, Vex's reputation | She keeps Elias's room above the back door; she knew him for years. |
| Vex | The salvage, Elias's fate, the fog's rules (partly) | He hides what he knows; what he lets slip must be forced out, not volunteered. |
| Rook | The first rule (sound), Elias's log | He was Elias's navigator; he is ashamed he did not sail. |
| Quill | Vex's crew, the fog's behaviour, the chart table | She watches; she does not explain the island's past. |
| Hobb | Tam, the bell, the lost crews | His brother Tam rang the bell; Hobb came to find him. |
| Dorrit | The stores, the crews, Vex's hold | He was blamed for the theft; he knows the harbour's gossip. |
| Pike | The boat shed, the stolen stores | He took them; he will not admit it until caught. |
| Elias | The three rules, Mordant, the *Arcadia* | He learned them on the fog rock; he gives them one at a time. |
| Mordant | His own name, the grey crews | He says what he is; he does not lie. |

**The rule:** a character may only name a fact the player has already been shown, or must be the
one who *sources* it in that scene. If Quill names Mordant before Elias has taught the name, the
line is wrong. If a stranger knows Fenn's father, the line must say how.

## Family-friendly, warm, never lewd

- The game is for families. Charming, warm, funny, eerie, sad — not sexy, not gory.
- Fenn and Quill: professional warmth and one chaste beat at most. No kiss, no body description,
  no jealousy plot. She is a navigator with her own contract, not a prize.
- Women are written by what they do, not how they look. Attractiveness lives in the portraits and
  in wit, competence and warmth. Nobody is described by their figure; no cleavage, no pin-up
  framing, no leering camera.
- The dead are sad, not disgusting. No rot, no blood, no screaming. The fog is quiet and patient.
- No paperwork plot. No insurance, no ledgers, no forms, no stamping. Vex's crime is salvage and
  silence, not an audit. Pike may be a jobsworth, but the joke must not become a plot about forms.
- No minors in any romantic frame. Tam the island kid stays a kid; Tam Hobb is an adult and is
  always named so.

## Before and after

Bad (constructed, explains itself):
`"The harbour keeps its grief in the ledgers, and the ledgers keep it quiet."`

Good (one idea, in character):
`"Half the harbour is empty. The other half won't meet my eye."`

Bad (a stranger knows too much):
`"Your father Ansel owed my brother a debt, as you well know."`

Good (sourced in the scene):
`"Elias Fenn. He charted the Shroud for me, before it took him."`

Bad (choice hides the action):
`"I could perhaps see my way to asking him about the arrangement."`

Good: `Ask Vex about the salvage.`

Bad (lewd or body-focused):
`"She turns, and the whole tavern forgets what it was saying."`

Good: `"She turns before I finish. 'Say it to my face.'"`
