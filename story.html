The premise

Fenn is a customs clerk in Port Vela, a harbour that has quietly stopped working.

Six ships in three years have gone into the Vela Straits and come back empty — sails still set, boats still hanging, and the wheel lashed amidships. The insurers call it weather. Everyone on the waterfront knows weather doesn't tie off a wheel.

Three years ago Fenn's father sailed for the Straits on the Arcadia and never came back. All that's left of him is a brass compass that has never once pointed north.

Tonight the Marrow sails for the Straits at dawn, and she only takes licensed hands. Fenn has until first light to get a berth, which means getting a licence, which means getting past a harbourmaster who has been signing the wrong things for years.

Three years stamping other people's voyages. At dawn the Marrow sails for the Straits, where my father's ship vanished — and she only takes licensed hands. Fenn · opening line

Act I — A Licence to Sail

A chain of small dishonesties, ending at the one that matters.

  1. No licence, no berth. Bramwick won't sign a clerk onto the Marrow, and every berth needs a Seafarer's Licence, stamped by him, on paper thick enough to stop a knife.
  2. The stamp is locked away. The form is on the desk. The stamp is in his drawer, and the key went to Marta at cards. Marta wants a lemon for the punch. The lemons are nailed shut in a crate on the pier, and there's a crowbar leaning against a bollard.
  3. The licence is stamped — and refused. Bramwick looks at it and stops. “Your father asked me for the same thing, three years ago. I signed it, and I have wondered ever since whether I should have.” He wants two more things: the Arcadia's manifest copy, and the truth out of the harbour ledger.
  4. The chandler's price. Mr Tallow will find any manifest you like — once his three-year pile of invoices is stamped paid. He speaks from the back room and is never seen.
  5. The loft. The harbour ledger is chained to a desk in the loft above the chandler's, behind a shutter bolted from the lane. The crowbar has a second job.
  6. The entry. The Arcadia was written off as lost at sea three days before she was due. The ink is Bramwick's. Under it, a harbour fee, paid in full — by a factor's office. Nobody pays harbour dues on a wreck.
  7. “I was paid, and I wrote her down as lost.” Shown the manifest and told what the ledger says, Bramwick signs the licence without reading it, and cancels Silas Cray's claim.
  8. Offered a trade. In the lane, Cray offers the berth for the compass. He knows what it is, and he'd rather buy it than take it. Refusing him costs nothing tonight, and will cost something later.

Act II — The Vela Straits

Dawn, and Fenn is standing on the deck of the Marrow with a licence and no sea legs. The crew have already decided what he is.

Quill won't let a clerk aft of the mast. Her spyglass went over the side in the dark and is lying between two lashed crates; return it and she forgets she ever saw him.

Up the steps is the quarterdeck, and behind the wheel is Captain Vane — who is steering south of the reef on the charts of a navigator who is dead, and does not want a clerk's opinion about it.

Her binnacle holds an honest ship's compass, swinging north. Fenn sets his father's beside it, and they disagree.

The needle swings past north as if north had insulted it, and stops on a bearing south of the reef — the one line Vane has refused to draw. Act II · the cabin

Before she can answer, the lookout's whistle cuts through the deck: two notes, then one. A wreck, and it's flying a flag.

What Act III is being set up to answer

One

What is the Lull?

Past the reef the water goes quiet — not calm, quiet. Twenty-two people off one street went into it. Onn's only instruction is the price of listening.

Two

Who paid Bramwick?

A harbour fee paid in full by a factor's office. Cray wanted the compass badly enough to buy a berth for it. He isn't collecting cargo. He's collecting a direction.

Three

What is still flying her flag?

The wreck is the Arcadia, three years out, sails set and crew gone — and something aboard is still doing the ship's paperwork.

Ends on the wreck, on a card

Port Vela itself: the docks, the customs house, the Salty Keel, Chandler's Lane, the chandler's shop, the harbour loft, and the village of Upper Town up the steps — which is pure exploration, with three neighbours to talk to and nothing to solve.

The Taunt Duel — the rhyming boast duel meant to be the series' signature mechanic — is designed but unwritten. It belongs to Act III.
